The world

OCTAThe eight-sided planet · The Dice System

The third world of Ruby’s Dice System: the d8, eight triangular sides, and nothing on it discovered or drawn yet.

Sides
Eight, named by the number on the die for now (Ruby, 2026-10-17: “the sides are just named by the number on the dice”), unless two more directions join Hexa’s six: see below. Side 1 is the first side. Opposite sides add up to 9, as on a d8, with 1, 3, 5 and 7 around the top point and 2, 4, 6 and 8 around the bottom one; how the numbers sit on the die is a reading.
Drawn so far
Nothing. Ruby: “so the first side hasent been discoverd or drawn yet”. Every side is blank until she discovers it.
The name
Octa, by a vote on 2026-10-17, two to one: see below.
Everything else
Unwritten: its size, how it turns, its star and fields, its moon, its day and its year, who went there. No star is drawn in its sky and the world does not turn on its own, because none of that is written yet. The clock in the dock is the Dice System’s shared clock, counted in days.

Two more directions?

Ruby: “unless you can preduce 2 more directions on top of Hexa's that fit in(give me at least 3 examples before we resort to the numberd situation)”. Hexa’s six are Canon, East, West, Far, North and South. Five pairs that could join them, none chosen; the sides stay numbered until she picks one or keeps the numbers:

  • Dawn and Dusk: the side where the day begins and the side where it ends. It fits the slow mornings and the twilight of these worlds.
  • Zenith and Nadir: straight up and straight down in the sky, the astronomer’s words. They fit the die’s two points, the top one and the bottom one.
  • Windward and Leeward: the side the wind comes from and the sheltered side, the sailor’s words. They fit a system of explorers and watery worlds.
  • Over and Under: the plainest pair. On Hexa, North is already the top and South the bottom, so these would have to mean the die’s two points instead.
  • Sunward and Starward: toward its own star, and out toward the far stars. They fit a planet whose place among the worlds is still to be written.

One more way, for the record: Hexa’s six directions are the six corners of an eight-sided die (the two shapes are each other’s inside out), so every side of Octa touches three of them, and a side could be called by its corners, the North-Canon-East side. That is a reading, offered, not used.
