The world

OCTAThe eight-sided planet · The Dice System

The third world of Ruby’s Dice System: the d8, eight triangular sides, turning under the same star with a d7 moon. A ship has landed on side 1; nothing is drawn yet.

Sides
Eight, named by the number on the die for now (Ruby, 2026-10-17: “the sides are just named by the number on the dice”), unless two more directions join Hexa’s six: see below. Side 1 is the first side. Opposite sides add up to 9, as on a d8, with 1, 3, 5 and 7 around the top point and 2, 4, 6 and 8 around the bottom one; how the numbers sit on the die is a reading.
Drawn so far
Nothing yet. Ruby, 2026-10-17: “so the first side hasent been discoverd or drawn yet”; 2026-10-18: “a ship as landed on side one of octa”. The landing is marked in the middle of side 1 (the spot is a reading); whose ship it is, where it came from and what it found are unwritten. Every side is blank until she draws it.
The name
Octa, by a vote on 2026-10-17, two to one, and by the rule Ruby gave the same day for every planet: “witchever is the hedron name minus "hedron"”. See below.
The star
Tetra’s dark purple star, the same for all three worlds (Ruby, 2026-10-17: “give octa the same star”). It stays where it is; turn the world to find it. Where Octa sits among the worlds, and how far from the star, is unwritten, so the star is drawn as large as from Hexa.
Turn
Octa turns (Ruby: “a turn”): one full turn a day, leftward, like Tetra and Hexa. The direction and the day’s length are readings; its days are the shared clock’s days.
Like the others
Ruby, 2026-10-18: “EVERYTHING IS SIMILAR TO THE FIRST TWO”. So Octa has the two fields, one magical and one magnetic (the gold and blue rings), rain in the rain season, Tetra’s plants, Tetra’s week, a 25-hour day, and years counted from the ship’s landing as Hexa counts from Amber’s: year 0 of Octa now (Ruby, 2026-10-18: “it shuld be year 0 of octa”). The magical field is stronger than Hexa’s (Ruby, 2026-10-18: “why is octa's magical field the same as hexa, it shuld be stronger”): four gold rings instead of three, thicker and brighter; how much stronger is a reading. The magnetic field is as Hexa’s. Where “similar” had to be read as “the same” (the plants, the week, the day’s length, the magnetic field’s strength, the rain months, counting the years from the landing) it is a reading.
The sky
Every other planet shows in Octa’s sky (Ruby, 2026-10-18: “you shuld be able to see all of the other planets from a given planet”): Tetra and Hexa as small turning dice nearest the star, the five not yet built as points of light with their names farther along, on one curved line from the star (Ruby, 2026-10-18: “Why are all the planets not in a curved line from te sun”), in the order she stacked the fields (2026-10-04: “if there were 5 magical fields, and tetra was in the first one, cuba would be in the last one (going away from the star would be the direction they were stacked in)”), nearest the star first. She placed only Tetra and Hexa; the page sorts the rest by die size, which puts Octa and the five beyond Hexa: a reading she may overrule. The line’s bend and where it runs across the sky are readings.
Unwritten
Octa’s size; whose ship landed, which way it lies and what it found; the fifteenth month’s name.

The calendar

Months
15 · 28 days each: Hexa’s fourteen and one more (Ruby, 2026-10-17: “a calender with one more month than hexa”). The fifteenth month sits between March and April and has no name yet (Ruby, 2026-10-18: “the 15th month goes between march and april, but is still unnamed”).
Year
420 days + the Restart Day, which on Octa is one and three quarters of a day long (Ruby: “a restart day that is one and three fourths of a day”): 421¾ days. The year begins on 1 January, after the Restart Day, as on the other two worlds (a reading).
Week
Seven days with Tetra’s names, as Hexa has them (a reading for Octa): Starday to Twilightday. 420 month days are exactly 60 weeks, so every month runs Starday to Twilightday and the Restart Day stands outside the week.
Seasons and years
Like Hexa’s (Ruby: everything is similar): the rain season over the Restart Day, January and the first half of Amberary, then the time between rain and spring, spring from February, summer from April, fall from July, winter from October. The fifteenth month is half and half, as Amberary is (Ruby, 2026-10-18: “the first half of the 15th month is spring, the secont half is in betwee nspring and summer”): spring to its 14th day, the time between spring and summer from its 15th, then summer from April. Years count from the ship’s landing (a reading, as Hexa counts from Amber’s): year 0 of Octa (Ruby: “it shuld be year 0 of octa”). The year before it is -0 and the one before that -1, as Tetra counts its years (a reading).

The moon

Ruby, 2026-10-17: “a d7 moon”. So Octa’s moon is a seven-faced die, and like the other two moons it is fair and no barrel (Ruby, 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-11): a short five-sided prism with five oblong sides carrying one to five craters and two pentagon ends carrying six and seven, its length picked so each of the seven faces takes a seventh of the view from the middle (the shape and the proportion are readings, as for Hexa’s d5). It is a third of Octa’s size and keeps the other moons’ ways: opposite the star on ordinary days, swinging round in front of the star on the 14th of every month for an eclipse, rolling once a day, waxing and waning through the month. That it keeps those ways is a reading; Ruby gave the die.

Two more directions?

Ruby: “unless you can preduce 2 more directions on top of Hexa's that fit in(give me at least 3 examples before we resort to the numberd situation)”, then “give me another 5 so they arent confusing”, then, 2026-10-18: “for the names, you do not realise that it rotates so that nay face could be seen”. She is right: a side that faces the star now faces away half a day later, so no name tied to the star, the light or the view can stick to a side. Dawn and Dusk, Sunrise and Sunset, Light and Dark, Inner and Outer, Sunward and Starward, Windward and Leeward are withdrawn, and Gold and Blue with them: the gold field lies toward the star and the blue away from it, so those two would move over the sides as well.

What does stay fixed on a turning die is its axis: the two points it turns about, and the ring of four sides round each. Hexa’s six are Canon, East, West, Far, North and South. Pairs that stick to the body, none chosen:

  • Zenith and Nadir: the point on top and the point underneath, the astronomer’s words.
  • Peak and Deep: the same two points in plain words.
  • Over and Under: the plainest pair; on Hexa, North is already the top and South the bottom, so these would mean the die’s two points instead.
  • Crown and Keel: the top of the die and its underside, the way a ship has a keel.
  • High and Low: the upper ring of four sides and the lower ring of four.
  • Upper and Lower: the same two rings, said plainly.

Ruby, 2026-10-18, later: “can i pleese have 5 more pairs that also dont have anything to do with up and down?” Five more that stick to the body without its two points, none chosen:

  • Hither and Yon: side 1, the ship’s landing, with the three sides that touch it (3, 4 and 7), and side 8 with the three that touch it (2, 5 and 6). Hither is the old word for “this way”, yon for “that way, over there”.
  • Lesser and Greater: the sides numbered 1 to 4 and the sides numbered 5 to 8. In the page’s layout each four share one half of the die, cut by a plane through its axis, not across it.
  • Spinward and Counterspin: the way the die turns and the way back round. Directions round the die rather than names for sides: “the side spinward of this one”.
  • Bow and Stern: the way the ship on side 1 points and the way behind it, as a ship names its ends. Which way the ship lies is hers to say.
  • Port and Starboard: the ship’s left and right once its bow is set, the sailor’s pair.

Odd and Even would be a sixth, but in the page’s layout the odd sides are the upper ring and the even sides the lower, so it would say the same as High and Low unless the numbers move.

For the record: on an eight-sided die a side is in the upper ring or the lower one, and sits between two of Canon, East, Far and West (Hexa’s six directions are the six corners of this die). So two new words would mark the rings, and a side would be called, for example, the High Canon-East side. If that is not what she means, the numbers stand.
